Stirling Creek Cottage

A timber and rammed earth house beside a running creek, five minutes' walk from Stirling village.

Twelve old pine trees made the walls.

The boards were cut from trees that once lined Victorian farm driveways, and each tree became one wall of the house.

Inside, under the curved ceiling

The ceiling is radial-cut Silvertop Ash, arched like the hull of an old wooden ship. Two sets of French doors open onto the deck.

The creek out the back

In winter the creek runs fast below the garden and you can hear it from inside, with the log fire going. In the warmer months the French doors open to the deck, and the jasmine comes in.

How it was built

The house went up in 2002. The family who built it designed it themselves, facing north so the winter sun reaches the rooms and the solar panels, and did much of the building with their own hands.

The cladding came from old pine trees that lined farm driveways in rural Victoria. A mill in Yarram cut them radially, into wedges like a cake, then into boards. Twelve trees came to the site, and the boards were numbered so each tree stayed together.

  • Ceiling: radial-cut Silvertop Ash, curved
  • Walls: rammed earth and double-glazed windows in cedar frames
  • Floors: Rose Gum boards
  • Power and heat: solar panels, solar hot water, and a wood fire with a wet back that helps heat the water
  • Car: an electric vehicle charger in the garage

The house was opened to the public for Sustainable House Day in 2015 and 2016.

Who else lives in the garden

Koalas in the gums, kookaburras and black cockatoos, the odd kangaroo, and a possum that comes for the vine outside the lounge window most evenings.

Five minutes to the village

A walking trail leads from the garden into Stirling, so you can walk to brunch, the pub and Restaurant Aptos, which got its Michelin star this year.
